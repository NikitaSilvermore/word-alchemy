The Chronicle

How a sentence works
  • There is one way to win: bring the rival to 0 life. Plain attacking is slow. Both of you have a lot of life, so matching a thing to a verb and hitting will take a long time by itself. Three things speed it up.
  • Discovery: each different reaction you make with fuse in a match adds 2 to every blow you strike at the rival, for the rest of the match.
  • Ruin: when a thing is destroyed by anything other than an attack verb (fire, poison, rust, void, scalding), its owner loses 4 life.
  • Smoke: at the start of your turn you lose 1 life for each of your things that is burning. So does the rival.
  • Tap words in your hand, and things or people on the board, to build a sentence: adjectives, a subject, a verb, an object.
  • A noun from your hand is brought onto your side. Nouns and verbs cost 1 breath per syllable; every adjective costs 1. You get 6 breath a turn.
  • A thing acts only through a verb. Strike works for anything with a body. Other verbs need a thing that suits them and hit harder or do stranger things: beasts bite, heavy things crush, sharp things cut, spirits haunt.
  • Attacks are aimed at the rival, never at their things. Strike, bite, crush, cut and sting go for the person. The rival then chooses whether one of their things steps in, takes the hit, and hits back if it survives. Stings, spirits and hidden attackers cannot be stopped.
  • Things remember their verbs. Once you speak a verb with one of your things, it knows that verb and can use it again every turn for no breath. A thing knows two verbs at most, and acts once a turn. The verb card itself goes back into your deck to be drawn again. Your opening hand always holds at least one thing and one attack.
  • Adjectives cost 1 breath on your own things and 3 on one of the rival’s. They ride along with attacks: a venomous thing poisons what it hits, a burning one sets it alight, a radiant one blinds it, an ancient or vast one hits harder, a winged one flies over defenders.
  • Other verbs can name a thing directly: burn, dissolve, poison, bind, blind, haunt, petrify, eat. So can a category with an adjective, such as frozen beasts.
  • Evolving verbs change a thing for good. Enchant with an adjective makes the adjective its nature: enchant burning owl makes a fire owl, stronger, whose blows set things alight. Frozen makes frost, venomous venom, winged sky, occult shade, and so on. Gild, temper, distil, ripen, tame, crown, awaken and name each change the right kind of thing. Multiples add to the vigour gained.
  • The ground. Elements and what they make (fire, water, earth, air, void, wind, steam, swamp, lava, dust, energy, cloud, life) are not things that fight. They go to your ground, a separate space of four beside your things. They cannot be attacked, and they do not attack, guard or block. They work by their nature each turn, they can be blown, drenched or burnt with, and they are what you join with fuse.
  • Spells have no body and do not fight. Single-use spells (loan, omen, echo, memory, dream) are said alone, happen once and are spent. Lasting spells (rune, nemesis, muse, time, hunger, ink, debt, rumour, plague) are said alone and stand beside the ground, working by themselves for four of their keeper’s turns, then fade. Breath can also be drunk at once for 2 breath. Debt, rumour and plague land on the rival. Spells cannot be attacked, and at most three stand on a side.
  • And makes things react. Say a thing, fuse, and another thing: the two are used up and become a third. Fire and water make steam, earth and fire make lava, lava and water make obsidian. Either thing can be a noun in your hand or already on the board, and the second can be one of the rival’s for 2 more breath. What is made has the vigour of both put together, and keeps their forms: frost lead and water make frost quicksilver. Some reactions need a condition, such as a fire burning on your side, and frozen things react only with fire. Words that will react with the one you have picked glow green. Every reaction you find is kept in your grimoire, below.
  • Things act by their nature, without being told. At the start of your turn fire spreads to something flammable opposite, water puts out a fire on your side, a cloud rains on something opposite, earth feeds your plants, air feeds your flames, void eats at the strongest thing opposite, swamp holds one thing fast, lava cools to stone, and steam and dust drift away. Each card says what its nature is.
  • Wind carries. Air blows fire, or wind blows anything of yours: it is carried across the rival and every thing on their side, and each is touched by what it is. Fire burns them all, water soaks them all, venom poisons them all, dust blinds them all. The thing carried stays yours.
  • Weather and seasons are spoken alone and change the whole board until another replaces them: rain feeds every plant, desert withers them, a storm sends lightning at metal.
  • Words do what they mean, and a sentence that makes no sense does nothing. A category such as beasts or mortals means every one on the board, yours included.
  • Opposites meet. A sentence that joins a word with its true opposite counts double: a winged thing that is heavy, fire laid on frost, the sacred on the foul, the quick on the sleeping, the hidden on the bright. One of the two must be a word you speak in that sentence. With a peacock on your side it counts three times.
  • The first saying lands hardest. The first time in a duel you send a given thing to attack in a given way, the blow is 2 harder. Say the very same sentence a third time and it is 1 softer; a fifth time, 2 softer.
  • Courage. At 10 life or less, the first sentence you speak each turn costs no ink, and nothing can step in front of it. The same is true of the rival.
  • Black matter. Each of your things that is destroyed leaves a mark. The next thing you bring forth rises out of it with 1 more vigour for each mark, up to 4.
  • Adjectives on the weather. Speak an adjective together with a weather and the word lies on everything the weather touches, on both sides, and on everything that arrives while it lasts: burning rain, venomous fog, sacred night.
  • Each sentence that works earns 1 ink, 3 a turn at most. After each turn five words from the whole lexicon are laid out to be scribed: scribe one and it goes straight into your hand for the rest of the match.
  • You keep your hand between turns. Between turns you can discard words, or banish them for 1 ink. Ink you have not spent when the match ends goes home with you.
  • Between matches spend ink at the oracle on new words, build your deck from the words you own, and refine your best words. Winning raises your rank, which unlocks rarer words, more breath and bigger decks.
